# Visualization

The game ships with a built-in analytics dashboard, **Playtesting Analytics**, implemented in `core/stat_viewer.py`. It is a standalone Tkinter window that the player opens from the main menu / pause menu via the **VIEW STATS** button. The dashboard reads five CSV log files written during gameplay and renders one matplotlib chart per metric.

The window has two dropdowns:

- **View** — switches between the five charts (Net Revenue, Customer Satisfaction, Customer Throughput, Assembly Accuracy, Ingredients Sold).
- **Range** — toggles between **Last 100** rows (recent play) and **All** rows (lifetime data).

There is **one visualization page** total — every chart shares the same window and the View dropdown swaps the image canvas. Six screenshots follow: one for the overall page, and one each for the five components.

## 1. Overall dashboard view

![Overall dashboard](dashboard_overview.png)

Single Tkinter window titled **Playtesting Analytics**. The dark-themed layout has a header (top), the two control dropdowns (View / Range), the active chart rendered into the central image canvas (820 × 500 px), and a small footer reminding the player to close the window to continue. Closing the window resumes the game; the call to `StatViewer.run()` blocks until then. The dropdowns are styled with the same dark palette as the chart bodies (deep navy `#0f3460` field, accent-pink `#e94560` arrows) so the controls and the chart read as one cohesive surface.

## 2. Net Revenue (line chart)

![Net Revenue chart](chart_net_revenue.png)

Line chart of cumulative money over time, generated by `_chart_revenue()`. The X-axis is the transaction number (one point per submitted burger), and the Y-axis is the player's money balance, formatted with a `$` prefix. The line is rendered in the accent pink (`#e94560`) with a translucent fill underneath (alpha 0.15) to make growth and dips easy to read at a glance. The title shows the active range (e.g. `Net Revenue — Last 100`). This chart answers questions like *"is the player making money over the course of a shift?"* and *"are there flat stretches where revenue stalls?"* — useful for tuning ingredient prices and shift difficulty.

---

## 3. Customer Satisfaction (pie chart)

![Customer Satisfaction chart](chart_customer_satisfaction.png)

Pie chart of star-rating distribution, generated by `_chart_satisfaction()`. Each wedge is one of the 0–5 star ratings, sized by how many customers received that rating. Wedges with at least 5% share have their percentage drawn directly on the slice; smaller wedges are kept clean and only labelled in the legend. The legend on the right lists every rating with the form `★ — count (percentage)`, so readers can see exact numbers even for tiny slices. This visualization shows whether the player is consistently delivering high-quality burgers or scraping by — a heavy 0–1 star slice means the rating-gate game-over (avg < 2 ★) is about to trigger.

---

## 4. Customer Throughput (bar chart)

![Customer Throughput chart](chart_customer_throughput.png)

Bar chart of how often each customer-count level was sampled, generated by `_chart_throughput()`. Throughput is logged every 10 seconds by `StatTracker.update()` as the total customers in the shop (ordering + waiting). The X-axis is the discrete customer count (0, 1, 2, …) and the Y-axis is how many sample windows had that count. A right-skewed distribution means the shop spent most of its time crowded; a left-skewed distribution means the player burned through the queue quickly. This is the primary signal for whether the difficulty curve is producing the intended "calm opening → rush hour" arc.

---

## 5. Assembly Accuracy (histogram with mean line)

![Assembly Accuracy chart](chart_assembly_accuracy.png)

Time-series bar chart of average customers in the shop at each half-hour of the shift, generated by _chart_throughput(). Throughput is sampled every 10 seconds by StatTracker.update() as the total customers in the shop (ordering + waiting). Samples are bucketed by their game_hour value and averaged within each half-hour window. The X-axis is the in-game hour (0 through 5.5) and always spans the full 6-hour shift regardless of how far the player got — buckets with no samples render as zero-height bars rather than disappearing — and the Y-axis is the average customer count for that bucket. This is the cleanest read on whether the difficulty curve is producing the intended "calm opening → rush hour → cool-down" arc — a flat chart means difficulty isn't ramping, while an early spike means the rush hits before the player has earned enough money to handle it.

---

## 6. Ingredients Sold (data table)

![Ingredients Sold table](table_ingredients_sold.png)

Table component (not a chart), generated by `_chart_ingredients()` using `matplotlib.Axes.table`. Each row is one ingredient ID, with five columns: **Ingredient**, **Total Units**, **Mean / Customer**, **Std Dev**, and **Total Revenue**. Rows are sorted by total units descending, so the most-used ingredient sits on top. The header row is highlighted in the accent pink, and body rows alternate between two dark-blue shades for readability. Aggregations are computed with `pandas.groupby` — `sum` for totals and revenue, `mean` and `std` for per-customer usage. This table directly answers stocking decisions: which ingredients to keep restocked, which carry the highest revenue contribution, and which are rarely used.

## File reference

- **Source code:** `core/stat_viewer.py`
- **CSV inputs** (under `data/gamedata/`):
  - `revenue_log.csv` → Net Revenue
  - `satisfaction_log.csv` → Customer Satisfaction
  - `throughput_log.csv` → Customer Throughput
  - `accuracy_log.csv` → Assembly Accuracy
  - `ingredients_log.csv` → Ingredients Sold
- **Generated chart PNGs** (under `data/gamedata/stat_img/`): one per metric, regenerated every time the View or Range dropdown changes.
